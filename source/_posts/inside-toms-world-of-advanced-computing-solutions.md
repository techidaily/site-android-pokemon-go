## The New Era of Budget Computing: Loongson CPU-Based Mini PC at Just $387

A Chinese-made Loongson 3A6000 SoC is making its way into the retail market in the[Morefine M700S](https://item.jd.com/10100670938060.html?cu=true&utm%5Fsource=kong&utm%5Fmedium=jingfen&utm%5Fcampaign=t%5F236375426%5FRV%5FmuIXmupA&utm%5Fterm=361a1a8983f343c79f91f41841f2d26c) mini PC. The CPU isn't a top-of-the-line performer compared to currently available AMD and Intel chips, though; The A36000 CPU's performance is equivalent to an[Intel Core i3-10100F](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/loongson-3a6000-beats-i3-10100f) and about Zen 3 levels of IPC.

 Loongson's chip has four cores and eight threads with clock speeds between 2.0 and 2.5 GHz. At max, it supports up to 64GB DDR4-3200 SO-DIMM memory. According to the manufacturer, the SoC is made for industrial use, government, education, medicine, and other fields.

[Based on earlier reports about this CPU's performance](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/loongson-launches-3a6000-cpu-matches-14600k-ipc) , it makes sense to pitch such a device towards these fields, which wouldn't need the highest-end performance but will appreciate a PC with a small footprint and adequate I/O options. The M700S retails for 2,799 Chinese Yuan, the equivalent of $387.

![Morefine M700S Specifications](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/FUgSdaYtfiWfkkZSH4ED38-320-80.png)

(Image credit: JD)

 The M700S looks like a NUC-style mini PC, measuring 149 x 145 x 50 mm, and weighs 812 grams. It supports 2.5G and 5G dual-band Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.0 with one PCIe 3.0 slot for an M.2 drive and a SATA connection for a 7 mm 2.5-inch drive. The built-in graphics supports a 4K resolution at 30 Hz and dual screens via two HDMI ports. The M700S has four USB 2.0 ports, two USB 3.0, and one USB Type-C with 30W PD 3.0\. For wired internet connection, it has one Gigabit Ethernet port and a 3.5 mm headphone jack for[audio](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/audio) .

 According to the listing, it is pre-installed with Linux-based[Loongnix OS](https://www.loongson.cn/EN/system/loongnix) but is compatible with many operating systems like Windows 10,[Windows 11](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/windows-11) , Tongxin UOS, Galaxy Kirin, and Ubuntu.

## Challenges and Opportunities in Sanction-Imposed Nations

 Making a CPU from scratch is no small feat. This CPU will likely be featured in desktops and[notebooks](https://www.tomshardware.com/tag/notebooks) intended for some fields. Loongson may make its CPUs[available in Russia](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/loongon-cpus-bound-for-russia-after-china-lifts-export-ban) and Belarus as the countries have trade sanctions imposed by the EU and the US. Russia is currently struggling with its chip-making venture; about half of its recently manufactured chips[are defective](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/half-of-russian-made-chips-are-defective-baikal-struggles-to-meet-russias-demand) . The country is also transitioning towards its locally made software since Microsoft is shutting down its[Office 365 cloud computing services](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/russian-businesses-get-shut-out-from-microsoft-cloud-services-at-the-end-of-this-month-new-eu-sanctions-come-into-effect) . Loongson may enjoy a certain market share in Russia for now because of these issues.

 Despite that, it will take time for companies like Loongson to make CPUs that rival Intel and AMD's performance. Intel's Pat Gelsinger has said the chipmaking companies in China are[ten years behind](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/manufacturing/intel-ceo-gelsinger-says-china-is-ten-years-behind-in-chipmaking-capabilities-and-it-will-stay-that-way) . But as China has been on a shopping spree for[chip-making tools](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/chinas-chipmaking-tool-purchases-skyrocket-imports-up-256-in-the-face-of-intensifying-us-sanctions) and with opportunities to do business in countries imposed with sanctions, we'll see if it's able to catch up.
